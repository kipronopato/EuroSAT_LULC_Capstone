      About the models

      DSA 8401 Applied Machine Learning capstone: land-use / land-cover classification of Sentinel-2 imagery.

      How a map is made

      The models classify one 64 × 64 pixel patch: 640 m × 640 m at Sentinel-2's 10 m resolution. To map a larger image, that window slides across it with overlap. Every pixel gets the average of the predictions of all windows covering it (weighted towards window centres), and takes the winning class.

      Images are rescaled first so a window always covers 640 m on the ground, because that is the scale the models learned.

      What answers your upload

      • 13-band GeoTIFF: a custom CNN on all 13 bands, averaged with EfficientNet-B0 on the true-colour bands, each over the 8 flips/rotations of the patch.
      • PNG / JPEG: EfficientNet-B0 (ImageNet-pretrained, fine-tuned), over the 8 flips/rotations.

      Both were trained on 19,286 EuroSAT patches and tested on 3,857 patches from places never seen in training (a spatial split).

      Good to know

      Patch-level, not pixel-exact. Boundaries are about 100–300 m wide; small features (a single building, a narrow stream) can be missed.

      Made for Sentinel-2. Drone photos or zoomed-in web maps look different from 10 m imagery; set the ground resolution and treat results with caution.

      European training data. EuroSAT covers 34 European countries; other landscapes (tropical farms, informal settlements) may be confused.

      Data

      EuroSAT (Helber et al., 2019), 27,000 labelled Sentinel-2 patches. API documentation for developers.
